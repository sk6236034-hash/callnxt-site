What it does

  • Tokens on WhatsApp. Patients book a token by day, get reminders, and can move or cancel it themselves. Typed Hindi and Hinglish are understood.
  • A queue that runs itself. Check-in by scanning the door QR, "your turn is near" alerts, and a waiting-room TV that calls out token numbers.
  • Prescriptions in seconds. Type two letters for medicines, tests and diagnoses, use your pre-built diagnoses, and send a PDF with your registration details on WhatsApp.
  • Fees by UPI or cash. Fees go straight to the clinic's own UPI ID, or are paid at the desk in cash or by UPI. Fee receipts with GSTIN, and earnings by day, week or month.
  • Reports and follow-ups. Patients send lab reports and X-ray photos; the clinic sends reports, certificates and referral letters back on WhatsApp.
  • Your own clinic page. A simple public page with your timings, fee and address, and a "Book on WhatsApp" button.
  • Works on a weak signal. The queue and prescriptions keep working offline and catch up when the internet is back.
  • Private by design. Each clinic's data is kept apart; patients can see or delete their data under India's DPDP Act.

Questions

Do my patients need to install an app?

No. They use the WhatsApp they already have. The live token page opens in any phone browser.

Which WhatsApp number do patients message?

The clinic's WhatsApp number is set up with you during onboarding, and printed on the QR poster for your door.

What if the internet goes off in the clinic?

The doctor screen keeps working offline: you can call patients and write prescriptions, and everything is sent when the connection returns.

Where is the data kept?

In India, with each clinic's data kept apart. Read the privacy policy for the details.

Can my assistant use it too?

Yes. The assistant gets their own sign-in for the queue, bookings and payments, without seeing clinical notes.
